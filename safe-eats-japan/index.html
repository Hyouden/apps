What she can eat

Japanese food is naturally low in dairy. The big enemy is soy sauce — it contains wheat and soy and is in almost everything. Think: raw, grilled, salted.

Set up before you fly

Pack & prep
  • Bring travel packets of gluten-free tamari (it's soy) or coconut aminos (soy-free). Most restaurants don't stock GF soy sauce — with your own, sashimi, sushi and plain grilled food all open up. Show the card first, then ask: 自分の醤油を使ってもいいですか？
  • Email hotels & any ryokan dinners ahead — Kaiseki is soy-sauce heavy but kitchens adapt well with notice. This note is more detailed than the card:
  • Install Find Me Gluten Free (GF) and HappyCow (vegan = dairy-free) and star the Places in this app in Google Maps.
  • Travel days: station bento (ekiben) are soy-sauce heavy. Grab salt onigiri, boiled eggs, fruit or roasted sweet potato from a konbini, or eat at Soranoiro in Tokyo Station.
  • Hotel breakfast buffets: plain rice, grilled salmon, fruit, salad and boiled eggs are usually safe. Skip miso soup, natto and anything glossy.
Drinks
✅ Usually fine
Water, green tea, hōjicha, sake (rice), shochu, highballs, umeshu, wine, black coffee
❌ Watch out
Beer (barley), barley tea 麦茶 (often served free!), matcha & café lattes, milk tea, soy-milk lattes
